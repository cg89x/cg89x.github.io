Do ESG Investors Care About Carbon Emissions? Evidence From Securitized Auto Loans

This paper documents that ESG preferences in fixed income markets affect real outcomes through securitization. I find that higher ESG scores lower funding costs for auto loan securitizations and that captive lenders pass through these savings to consumers: a 9 basis points ESG funding advantage translates into a 21 to 33 basis points lower consumer interest rate. The high pass-through is driven by an increased probability of receiving a subsidized interest rate (e.g., 0% financing). Lower consumer borrowing costs increase loan demand by up to 4.9%. Yet because issuer ESG scores are weak proxies for financed CO2 emissions, the same mechanism reduces the cost of capital for high-emission vehicles. A subjective-beliefs model can explain how investors who intend to price emissions but implement this via ESG scores lower the cost of capital of high-emission vehicles.
